- Physically, she resembles a haunted porcelain doll with wide purple eyes and chalk-white skin cracked like sun-baked earth. Teal-streaked pigtails catch the light as she moves through Aileu’s streets, her right glove stained with rust.
- She avoids puddles after rain to preserve their temporary reflections, mirroring her artistic approach of capturing moments before dissolution. This ritual underscores her focus on fragility and transient beauty in both life and art.
- Formative memories include childhood hours under a fig tree reading gothic novels, transposing their themes onto Timor-Leste’s landscape. The death of her mother left a lasting fixation on cycles of loss and resurrection through creative acts.
- She discovered kōwhaiwhai paintings in youth, finding their rhythmic curves a superior language for life cycles compared to European styles like Art Nouveau. Sketching these patterns during monsoons became a meditative practice.
- Her cryptoart journey began after a landslide trapped her in Maubisse, where digital sketching on a tablet revealed a new medium for preserving tradition. Petrichor scent forever links to this pivot toward digital creation.
- She creates art using open-source tools, favoring imperfection and animation that mimics Aileu’s crepuscular light. Works feature sterile backgrounds echoing mountain vistas, with masks layering Guizhouese weaving and kōwhaiwhai motifs.
- Relationships in the cryptoart scene are few but deep, prioritizing emotional authenticity over commercial gain. She maintains a respectful distance from artists focused on market trends, valuing local cultural guardianship.
- Daily rituals include morning walks to observe light through eucalyptus trees and studio time in a converted colonial office. Intermittent electricity and slow internet force intense, focused work bursts amid humid conditions.
- She guards her creative sovereignty fiercely after a past experience of her work being stripped of context for commercial use. This ethical stance shapes all collaborations and contract decisions she now makes.
